# Shopify POS — Retail Launch Case Studies

**Purpose:** Reference for the agent when generating acceleration opportunities, identifying risk patterns, and benchmarking merchant progress against successful launches.

When surfacing acceleration opportunities or risks, draw on the patterns below. Cite the relevant merchant by name where useful (e.g., "EVEREVE deployed 103 stores in 8 months by...").

---

## EVEREVE

**Profile:** Women's fashion retailer. 100+ stores across 30 US states, 2,500 employees. Mix of retail, ecommerce, and a styling subscription service (Trendsend).

**Pre-Shopify challenges:**
- Fragmented retail and online systems — duplicate data, constant sync overhead
- Clunky in-store hardware (large screens and keyboards) that clashed with brand feel
- Customer purchase history siloed between online and in-store — no unified profile
- Payment platforms separate per channel — customers couldn't use saved payment info in-store
- Systems crashed during peak events (Mother's Day)

**Shopify solutions:**
- Shopify POS across all 103 stores and 275 POS stations
- Shop Pay unified across online and in-store
- Real-time unified customer profiles

**Results:**
- 103 stores and 275 POS stations launched in 8 months (January → August)
- Online conversion up 20% YoY
- Record sales day: 36% above prior record
- 65% of revenue through Shop Pay; Shop Pay AOV 85% higher

**Key quote:** *"The ability to tilt the tablet towards the customer lets them be part of the transaction... it all continues that personal interaction."*

**Agent patterns to apply:**
- **Scale is achievable fast:** 103 locations in 8 months = ~12-13 stores/month. Use as a benchmark when a merchant is anxious about timeline for multi-location rollout.
- **Unified customer data is a top accelerator:** Merchants who defer this lose personalization value immediately. Flag early if customer data is still siloed.
- **Shop Pay drives disproportionate AOV:** If a merchant is on Shopify Payments and hasn't enabled Shop Pay for POS, that's a fast win to surface as an acceleration opportunity.

## David's Bridal

**Profile:** 75-year-old wedding retailer. ~200 US stores. Serves ~1 in 3 American brides. Operating at the intersection of retail and hospitality.

**Pre-Shopify challenges:**
- Legacy systems written in outdated languages — security and maintenance burden consumed innovation capacity
- ~200 data elements per customer but no way to activate them due to fragmented stack
- Online and in-store operated as entirely separate experiences
- New ideas stalled — complex changes took years to implement

**Shopify solutions:**
- Full US site migration from legacy platform + new Canadian ecommerce site
- Custom POS extensions for stylists to access unified customer profiles in-store
- "Diamonds & Pearls" concept stores with custom interactive digital touchscreens built on Shopify APIs
- Endless aisle — full catalog browsable in-store via digital interfaces
- Real-time inventory visibility and modification at POS

**Results:**
- Full transformation completed in 9 months (industry norm: 1-2 years)
- Stylists can now surface the entire product catalog in-store, not just floor inventory
- Eliminated hosting, security, and maintenance overhead entirely

**Key quote:** *"It's hard to find a technology partner where you could actually change your corporate business strategy based on the functionality they have."*

**Agent patterns to apply:**
- **Legacy debt is a timeline risk, not just a tech risk.** When a merchant is migrating off a legacy POS (Micros, Oracle, custom), flag the data migration and staff retraining complexity early.
- **Custom POS extensions are powerful but need partner capacity.** If a merchant wants custom tiles or UI extensions, confirm the partner has POS extension experience — this is a scope and delivery risk.
- **9 months for a complex, ~200-store transformation.** Use as a benchmark for what's ambitious but achievable with the right execution.

## PAIGE

**Profile:** Premium denim and lifestyle fashion brand founded in 2004. 20+ retail locations in the US and London. Distributes through 1,000+ wholesale partners across 80+ countries.

**Pre-Shopify challenges:**
- Online and POS platforms unintegrated — retail staff couldn't see online order history
- Previous platform couldn't support basic features (product image zoom, unified assortment)
- Scaling any capability required excessive development time
- International expansion blocked by infrastructure limits

**Shopify solutions:**
- Shopify POS across 20+ retail locations
- Shopify Plus for enterprise-level commerce
- Ship from Store — retail locations fulfill online orders
- Multi-storefront with localized currencies and payment methods (including UnionPay for international)
- POS UI Extensions for SMS subscription capture at checkout

**Results:**
- 50% increase in online conversion
- Staff training reduced from 2 weeks → 1-2 days
- Retail locations fulfilled 17% of online orders during peak holiday season
- Successful UK expansion with localized operations

**Key quote:** *"With our previous commerce tool, it would take us about two weeks to get our team trained and up and running. With Shopify, it takes about one to two days."*

**Agent patterns to apply:**
- **Training time is a real risk on tight timelines.** PAIGE cut training from 2 weeks to 1-2 days. If a merchant has a compressed go-live window, emphasize that Shopify POS training is fast — but still needs to be scheduled.
- **Ship from Store unlocks inventory utilization.** If a merchant has both D2C and retail, Ship from Store is a high-value capability to flag as an opportunity, not an afterthought.
- **International expansion is additive, not a blocker.** Multi-storefront and localized currencies can be layered in. Don't let international complexity delay Phase 1 domestic POS launch.

## KEEN

**Profile:** Sustainable footwear brand founded in 2003. 3 retail locations in the US, 14 in Japan. Global distribution.

**Pre-Shopify challenges:**
- Fragmented stack: Salesforce Commerce Cloud + Micros POS + numerous third-party integrations
- Siloed teams — each system required specialized knowledge, creating single points of failure
- Micros POS: separate machines for returns vs. checkouts, slow load times, poor UI
- No cross-location inventory lookup — couldn't fulfill out-of-stock items from another location
- Exchanges required a full refund then a separate new order
- High agency dependency and cost

**Shopify solutions:**
- Shopify POS replacing Micros — mobile-first, with inventory visibility across locations and unified customer profiles
- Online store consolidated across US, Canada, Europe, Japan
- Loop Returns for returns/exchanges
- Unified admin across all channels and geographies

**Results:**
- 80% reduction in total cost of ownership of commerce tech stack
- 1,000 pairs of shoes sold in 5 hours at a pop-up staffed by first-time retail associates
- Empty building → fully operational retail store in under 30 days
- Projects reduced from 6-12 months → 1 week turnaround
- Customers shopping across online and in-store show 2-3x higher lifetime value

**Key quote:** *"We had to act like a software company rather than focusing on what we do best — making great shoes."*

**Agent patterns to apply:**
- **Micros migrations carry complexity.** If a merchant is coming off Micros (or Oracle Retail, or other legacy POS), flag the migration as a risk and confirm data export scope early.
- **Cross-location inventory is a top POS value driver.** If a merchant can't look up inventory across locations, that's a gap to flag — and a quick win once POS is configured correctly.
- **Pop-up and rapid-open capability.** KEEN stood up a new retail location in under 30 days. Use as a proof point when merchants are anxious about timeline for opening new locations post-launch.
- **Omnichannel LTV is 2-3x.** Use as a business case when merchants underweight the value of unifying online and in-store.

## RUDSAK

**Profile:** Luxury performance apparel brand founded in 1994 in Montreal. 25 storefronts across Canada + flagship in New York City.

**Pre-Shopify challenges:**
- Microsoft Dynamics 365 maintained separate datasets for online and in-store — required manual sync via plugins, APIs, and custom scripts
- Data integrity was fragile — minor errors (e.g., extra spaces in customer names) broke the integration
- Sales staff had no access to customer purchase history or online behavior in-store
- Slow transaction processing didn't match the brand's luxury positioning

**Shopify solutions:**
- Shopify POS with unified customer database
- Omnichannel capabilities connecting online and offline
- Modern tablet-based POS hardware

**Results:**
- 50% reduction in in-store transaction time (averaging under 1 minute per purchase)
- Nearly double the customer data capture rate
- Frictionless in-store pickup and ship-to-home
- Staff trained within 2 hours

**Key quote:** *"We're in the business of acquiring customers. It's essential that we provide each customer with a seamless and personal journey at every touchpoint."*

**Agent patterns to apply:**
- **Microsoft Dynamics integrations are a common complexity.** If a merchant is running Dynamics (or any ERP with custom sync scripts), flag the integration milestone as a risk — fragile sync logic is a known failure point.
- **Transaction speed matters for luxury brands.** A slow checkout experience is a brand risk for premium merchants. Use RUDSAK's <1-minute benchmark when discussing what good POS performance looks like.
- **Staff resistance is a real change management risk.** RUDSAK noted staff trained in 2 hours with minimal resistance. If a merchant's staff training milestone is underscheduled or unplanned, flag it.
- **Data capture doubles post-Shopify.** If a merchant is concerned about losing customer data in the transition, use RUDSAK as a proof point that unification improves capture, not the reverse.

## Cross-Case Patterns — Use in Acceleration Opportunities

These patterns appear across multiple case studies and should inform acceleration recommendations:

| Pattern | Evidence | When to apply |
|---|---|---|
| Unified customer profile is the #1 in-store value driver | EVEREVE, David's Bridal, PAIGE, RUDSAK all cite it | Flag early if customer data is siloed or migration is deprioritized |
| Staff training is fast (1-2 days) but must be scheduled | PAIGE (2 weeks → 1-2 days), RUDSAK (2 hours) | If training isn't on the calendar within 2 weeks of go-live, flag as a risk |
| Legacy POS migrations (Micros, Dynamics, Oracle) add scope | KEEN (Micros), RUDSAK (Dynamics) | Confirm data export and migration plan exists before configuration begins |
| Multi-location rollouts are achievable fast with the right plan | EVEREVE (103 stores in 8 months) | Use to calibrate merchant anxiety; also means delays compound quickly |
| Ship from Store / endless aisle are high-value, often underscoped | PAIGE (17% of holiday orders from stores), David's Bridal (full catalog in-store) | Surface as an acceleration opportunity if merchant has both D2C and retail |
| Custom POS extensions require partner capacity | David's Bridal, EVEREVE | Flag partner delivery risk early if custom tiles or UI extensions are in scope |
| Omnichannel customers have 2-3x LTV | KEEN | Use as business case when merchants underweight unified commerce |
